A release decision usually ends in a status meeting because Engineering,
Security, and Support each hold a different piece of the truth. Centralizing all
of that context is invasive and often impossible. ProofMesh turns the meeting
into a permission-scoped verification protocol.

Here, a release owner asks one precise question: is version 2.4 ready for
production? Before contacting anyone, ProofMesh scopes the decision, its
deadline, and the workspace boundary.

When I run the verification, ProofMesh uses Aicoo to route bounded requests to
owner-controlled agents for Engineering, Security, and Support. Each agent
answers independently from only the context its owner permits. The underlying
private source material is never centralized in ProofMesh.

Engineering verifies that all 247 continuous-integration checks passed.
Security verifies that the dependency audit has no critical findings. But
Support finds a contradiction: the rollback playbook still references a
retired queue.

ProofMesh does not smooth over that disagreement or invent consensus. It links
every verdict to its evidence, shows confidence, and lowers the overall proof
score. That contradiction is the only part sent to a human release owner.

Once the owner confirms the rollback playbook update, the Support verdict is
verified and the proof score reaches one hundred. The decision is sealed with
explicit human accountability.

Every agent check and the final human decision keeps a receipt in the proof
ledger, so the team can explain not only what shipped, but why it shipped.

ProofMesh depends on Aicoo's agent messaging, scoped context, and persistent
workspace. The server can route synchronous, idempotent requests directly to
three permitted team agents. Aicoo's coordinator handles the same decision when
individual contacts are not configured, while accumulate and tool discovery
extend the protocol into durable records and connected systems.

This is more than a chatbot. It is a trust layer for cross-functional work:
agents collaborate, owners stay in control, and release confidence becomes a
verifiable protocol.
